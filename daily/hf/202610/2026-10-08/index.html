1. Questioning the Questions: Sustaining Self-Evolution in Reasoning Models

Abstract:Self-evolving reasoning models learn from their own generated questions, yet repeated self-training can lead to performance collapse. In this paper, we investigate why performance deteriorates over successive rounds and how to sustain self-evolution. Our analysis identifies two recurring quality problems in self-generated questions: invalid questions and repeated variants of the same mathematical questions. First, invalid questions become more prevalent across rounds, and answer-consistency filtering further increases their proportion in training data. Second, existing question diversity controls based on lexical similarity can miss mathematically equivalent questions expressed in different ways, which leads to question diversity collapse in later training rounds. Building on these findings, we introduce R-Quest, which uses question validity and novelty feedback to guide self-evolution. We first train the solver to recognize and reject invalid questions, then use its judgments to guide questioner rewards and filter solver training data. To avoid question repetition, we use a frozen base model to compare sampled question pairs and provide novelty feedback. Empirically, our method consistently achieves the highest average performance on 12 benchmarks in mathematical reasoning, general-domain reasoning, and code generation across two model families. Additionally, R-Quest maintains stable performance gains over ten rounds of self-evolution, peaking in the final round and outperforming R-Zero by 17.32 points.

摘要：自我进化推理模型通过自身生成的问题进行学习，但反复自我训练可能导致性能崩溃。本文研究了为什么性能会在连续轮次中下降以及如何维持自我进化。我们的分析识别了自生成问题中两类反复出现的质量问题：无效问题和同一数学问题的重复变体。首先，无效问题在各轮次中变得更加普遍，并且答案一致性过滤进一步增加了其在训练数据中的比例。其次，现有基于词汇相似度的题目多样性控制可能遗漏用不同方式表达但数学等价的问题，这导致后续训练轮次中题目多样性崩溃。基于这些发现，我们提出了R-Quest，它利用问题有效性和新颖性反馈来引导自我进化。我们首先训练解题模型识别并拒绝无效问题，然后利用其判断来指导提问者奖励并过滤解题模型的训练数据。为避免问题重复，我们使用冻结的基础模型对抽样的问题对进行比较，并提供新颖性反馈。从实证结果来看，我们的方法在两类模型中在数学推理、通用领域推理和代码生成的12个基准上始终实现了最高平均性能。此外，R-Quest在十轮自我进化中保持了稳定的性能提升，在最后一轮达到顶峰，并比R-Zero高出17.32分。

2. nanoMuse: An Open-Source Personal Agent for Every Device You Own

Abstract:Assistants from 2011 answered and waited, and agents from 2023 did a task and stopped. In September 2026 Meta’s Muse showed an agent for one person, with accounts, devices, memory and a conversation that lasts, closed, in a vendor’s cloud, in one country. Such an agent is expected to act on a person’s accounts and devices, remember them across weeks, speak first when it is worth it, and answer for what it did. It is a kind of software, not a model, and until now had no open counterpart. This report defines the personal agent in five questions and three horizons. It reads how Muse is built from Meta’s public record and a copy of its production prompt, each statement marked by its source. It then presents nanoMuse, the open-source counterpart under the GPL-3.0, one agent on every device a person owns, with hands on the phone’s screen and the computer’s. They share one conversation over a relay anyone can run; every action goes through a Sentinel, memory is files the person can read, and the model is their choice. Its size and cost are given as estimates. What is open, memory with provenance, an evaluation suite for the hands and an open model for them, is set out as a roadmap.

6. ReSAIL: Mitigating Collapse in Iterative Agent Self-Distillation

Abstract:Iterative self-distillation enables LLM agents to learn from successive deployments, offering a path toward recursive self-improvement (RSI). Yet our experiments with existing methods reveal a collapse in deployment performance across cycles, while task performance with privileged information (PI) also declines. We address this collapse by prioritizing informative interaction steps for distillation and preserving PI-conditioned behavior as the student becomes the next teacher. We introduce Retentive and Selective Augmentation for Iterative Self-Distillation (ReSAIL), a plug-in augmentation for iterative PI-based self-distillation. ReSAIL selects interaction steps where PI most strongly changes the teacher’s predictions and balances the resulting distillation losses across trajectories. It also regularizes the student’s PI-conditioned output distributions toward those of the frozen teacher at selected and unselected steps to preserve PI-conditioned behavior for supervision in the next cycle. On ALFWorld and TextCraft, ReSAIL sustains substantial gains across model scales over three cycles, with an average absolute gain of 22.5% in final-cycle success rates when added to self-distillation baselines. Sensitivity-guided selection of offline data also improves action prediction accuracy for multimodal GUI agents on AITZ. These findings provide the first evidence that a more robust learning mechanism can effectively mitigate performance collapse in iterative agent self-distillation over deployment trajectories.

7. WorldSonus: Bringing Sound to Worlds

Abstract:Recent advances in world models have enabled increasingly realistic visual synthesis. However, these generated environments remain largely silent. Bringing sound to world models poses three core challenges: real-time generation to keep pace with interactive video streams, interactive control to respond to mid-stream sound instructions, and spatially aligned stereo to reflect scene geometry and camera motion. To address these demands, we introduce WorldSonus, an interactive video-to-audio framework designed for real-time spatial sound synthesis in world models. For real-time generation, WorldSonus employs a streaming causal autoregressive diffusion architecture that synthesizes audio chunks at a low real-time factor (RTF) of 0.41. For interactive control, we incorporate an audio-centric captioning pipeline with chunk-indexed prompt scheduling, enabling dynamic manipulation of sound events during generation. For spatial alignment, we leverage high-quality stereo supervision curated from diverse stereo and ambisonic data. Extensive experiments demonstrate that while tailored for world models, WorldSonus generalizes effectively to open-domain video-to-audio benchmarks, matching or outperforming state-of-the-art bidirectional models in both acoustic quality and spatial alignment. 8. Long-WAM: Scaling the Context of World-Action Models

Abstract:Real-time robot control demands enough visual history to infer motion and task progress, but processing that history can delay action. We present Long-WAM, a model-system framework for scaling the context of causal world-action models under real-time control constraints. Our central finding is that access to history is not the same as using it: longer histories pay off far more when the video foundation is pretrained autoregressively (AR). We first learn causal prediction from robot and egocentric videos without action labels, then preserve this history-to-future structure during world-action adaptation. On RoboCasa GR-1, increasing context from 0.0 to 19.2 seconds raises success from 63.3% to 78.7%, whereas a bidirectionally pretrained initialization shows no net gain; robot-domain AR pretraining further raises peak success on GR-1 and LIBERO-Long. Long-WAM also achieves the best results among compared methods on LIBERO-Long, RoboTwin 2.0, and DOMINO. Streaming observation encoding, asynchronous execution, and hardware-specific acceleration enable deployment on RTX 5090, DGX Spark, and Jetson AGX Thor without dropping future prediction; on RTX 5090, each action chunk, including future-video latent prediction, takes 107.4 ms. Real-time deployment on Unitree G1 and YAM supports dynamic and long-horizon manipulation, including 95% success on dynamic cup stacking, where Pi0.5 and Fast-WAM succeed in none of 20 trials. As a memory-informed executor, Long-WAM also complements higher-level planning in composite tasks.

9. Recursive Game Creator: An Agentic Product-Level Experience-Oriented Game Harness

Abstract:Recent game design agents have made substantial progress in generating playable games. However, program correctness does not ensure an enjoyable experience for players. We present Recursive Game Creator, an experience-oriented harness to advance agentic game development from rough game prototypes into entertaining games. Recursive Game Creator organizes recursive development around four components: Designer, Builder, Player, and Reviewer. The Designer translates user instructions and Reviewer’s feedback into detailed plans. The Builder turns these plans into candidate games. The coding-native Player creates and executes reusable policies through programmatic interfaces to efficiently collect diverse gameplay trajectories, mitigating evaluation bias caused by slow GUI-based collection. The Reviewer uses carefully designed trajectory-based metrics to induce player preferences, integrating with visual evidence and explicit textual preferences to evaluate games against game-specific criteria. Finally, the Reviewer accepts the better version and provides improvement reviews for the next round, closing the recursive loop. Our method achieves state-of-the-art overall performance of 77.89 on GameCraft-Bench. On GameASG-Bench, it achieves a strict task success rate of 53.2%, a 34.1% improvement over the same-model baseline, and the highest mean runtime-check pass rate at 93.4% among compared methods. A user study shows longer playtime and higher ratings. Code is coming soon.

10. Mechanics of Long-Context Hybrid Models Part 1.1: From Hybrid Attention to Hybrid Position

Abstract:The architectural design of Large Language Models (LLMs) is shifting from traditional full-attention-only models to hybrid models, which combine different attention modules to improve long-context efficiency and performance in length extrapolation and context extension. To explain why hybrid models work and how to design them better, we propose Mechanics of Long-Context Hybrid Models. As Part 1.1 of this series, we begin with hybrids of full attention and either sliding-window attention (SWA) or gated variants of linear attention (LA), represented by GLA and GDN. We first observe a Seesaw Effect in Context Extension: LA hybrids benefit more from long-context continual pretraining, whereas SWA hybrids perform better under length extrapolation. We attribute this behavior to differences in the positional inductive biases induced by these attention mechanisms. We find that SWA hybrids suffer from a Short-Context Learning Trap, Short-Window Weariness, and Long-Window Laziness, and require extended windows to enhance performance in continual long-context pretraining. For LA hybrids, we summarize the Matthew Effect of Hybrid Position Extrapolation and propose Sliding-Window Linear Attention, achieving 16$\times$ training-free length extrapolation while maintaining 100\% accuracy on NIAH-SK1 in 64k context length.

11. Recurrent Looped Transformer

Abstract:State tracking requires an update at every input, but the depth a Transformer applies to each token is fixed regardless of sequence length. We introduce the Recurrent Looped Transformer (RLT), which splits its layers between a parallel causal encoder and a recurrent decoder. At each token, the decoder merges the encoder output with the previous token’s final decoder state, so the computation path grows with sequence length at a fixed per-token cost. On six algorithmic tasks, we compare five splits of eight layers with an eight-layer Transformer over three seeds. Trained on at most 40 bits, two RLT splits generalize parity to 256 bits with 100% accuracy in every seed, while the Transformer stays at chance. On swap-based $S_5$ permutation tracking at eight times the training length, RLT reaches 97% final-state accuracy versus under 1% for the Transformer, and accuracy increases with decoder depth. On modular arithmetic beyond the training lengths, RLT reaches up to 93% versus 33% for the Transformer. Ablations show that these gains depend on the feedback: removing it drops parity and swap-based $S_5$ to chance at every split. Updating the feedback once per four-token chunk lets known tokens in a chunk run in parallel and keeps 64-bit parity at 99%, while permutation tracking depends on per-token feedback: chunking lowers length-64 swap-based $S_5$ from 100% to 20%.

中文摘要

摘要：状态跟踪需要在每次输入时进行更新，但 Transformer 对每个 token 应用的深度是固定的，与序列长度无关。我们引入了循环循环 Transformer（RLT），它将层拆分为并行因果编码器和循环解码器。在每个 token 上，解码器将编码器输出与前一个 token 的最终解码器状态合并，因此计算路径随着序列长度增长，同时每个 token 的计算成本保持不变。在六个算法任务中，我们将八层的五种拆分与八层 Transformer 在三个随机种子上进行比较。在最多 40 位的训练下，两个 RLT 拆分能够将奇偶性泛化至 256 位，在每个种子上准确率均为 100%，而 Transformer 仅为随机水平。在基于交换的 $S_5$ 排列跟踪任务中，在训练长度的八倍时，RLT 达到 97% 的最终状态准确率，而 Transformer 不到 1%，且准确率随着解码器深度增加。在超出训练长度的模运算上，RLT 达到最高 93%，而 Transformer 仅为 33%。消融实验表明，这些提升依赖于反馈：去掉反馈会使每种拆分下的奇偶性和基于交换的 $S_5$ 回到随机水平。每四个 token 更新一次反馈可以使 chunk 中已知的 token 并行运行，并保持 64 位奇偶性在 99%，而排列跟踪依赖于每个 token 的反馈：分块会将长度为 64 的基于交换的 $S_5$ 准确率从 100% 降至 20%。

Q: 这篇论文试图解决什么问题？ 这篇论文旨在解决\*\*固定深度Transformer在算法状态跟踪任务上的根本性长度泛化局限\*\*。 具体而言，论文识别并试图克服以下核心矛盾： - \*\*状态跟踪需要随序列增长的计算路径\*\*：算法任务（如奇偶校验、模运算、排列组合）要求模型在接收每个新输入时更新内部状态。理想情况下，处理 t 个token后，模型应经历了与 t 成比例的计算深度，以逐步累积或组合状态信息。 - \*\*Transformer的深度与序列长度解耦\*\*：标准Transformer对每个token施加固定数量的层，无论序列多长，每个位置的计算路径长度不变。除非 TC^0 = NC^1 ，否则固定深度的Transformer理论上无法在任意长序列上跟踪如 S_5 排列组合这类需要迭代状态更新的计算。 为突破这一限制，论文提出 \*\*Recurrent Looped Transformer (RLT)\*\*，其核心设计思想包括： 1. \*\*编码器–解码器分层\*\*：将固定总数的层拆分为并行的因果编码器（causal encoder）和循环解码器（recurrent decoder）。 2. \*\*跨token状态反馈\*\*：在每个位置，解码器通过门控合并（gated merge）将当前编码器输出与\*\*前一个token的最终解码器隐状态\*\*结合，使解码器状态沿序列方向循环传递。 3. \*\*随长度增长的等效深度\*\*：虽然每个token只通过 L_E + L_D 层，但处理 t 个token后，解码器状态经历了 t · L_D 个块的循环路径，从而在不增加单token推理成本的前提下，使有效计算深度随序列长度线性增长。 实验表明，在奇偶校验、基于交换的 S_5 排列跟踪和模运算等任务上，RLT在远超训练长度的测试序列上仍保持高精度，而同等层数的decoder-only Transformer则迅速退化至随机水平。 Q2: 有哪些相关研究？ 与 Recurrent Looped Transformer (RLT) 相关的研究可按照技术路线分为以下几类，主要对应论文第4节（Related Work）及引言中的理论讨论： ### 1. 固定深度 Transformer 的理论局限 - \*\*Merrill et al. (2024)\*\* 证明了除非 TC^0 = NC^1 ，否则固定深度的 Transformer 无法在任意长序列上跟踪 S_5 排列的组合。这为 RLT 引入随序列长度增长的循环计算路径提供了理论动机。 - \*\*Grazzi et al. (2024)\*\* 研究了线性 RNN 中通过负特征值解锁状态跟踪能力，其 S_5 状态跟踪任务被 RLT 用作评估基准之一。 ### 2. Transformer–RNN 混合架构 - \*\*Chen et al. (2018)\*\* 将 Transformer 编码器与基于 LSTM 的 RNMT+ 解码器结合用于机器翻译。RLT 同样采用“并行编码 + 循环解码”的混合思路，但 RLT 的解码器由带交叉注意力和滑动窗口注意力（SWA）的 Transformer 块构成，并以因果语言模型方式同时处理 prompt 和 response。 ### 3. 编码器衍生记忆复用 - \*\*YOCO (Sun et al., 2024)\*\* 为上层交叉解码器构建可复用的 KV 缓存，并支持 prefill 阶段的提前退出。 - \*\*DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (DeepSeek-AI, 2026)\*\* 从最终编码器状态投影全局解码器 KV，并维护独立的解码器 SWA。 - 与上述工作不同，RLT 的解码器\*\*必须按顺序处理每个 prompt token\*\*，因为其状态更新依赖于前一 token 的解码器输出，而非仅复用静态的编码器记忆。 ### 4. 跨 Token 的时序反馈机制 - \*\*Feedback Transformer (Fan et al., 2020)\*\* 在各层之间构建共享的反馈记忆，使后续 token 能在每层注意该记忆。RLT 则将前一 token 的最终解码器输出反馈到当前解码器输入的门控合并（gated merge）中，并辅以独立的编码器全局记忆与逐层 SWA 缓存。 - \*\*Recurrent Transformer (Oncescu et al., 2026)\*\* 在每一层维护持久的 KV 状态并提供精确的调度以优化显存搬运。RLT 的反馈跨越\*\*整个解码器深度\*\*，而 Recurrent Transformer 的反馈是逐层独立的。 ### 5. 隐态跨 Token 传递 - \*\*Full-bandwidth Transformer (Wang et al., 2026)\*\* 通过门控线性单元（GLU）将前一时刻顶层隐态与下一 token 嵌入结合，保留 Transformer 堆栈和 KV 缓存，并采用多轮训练实现并行教师强制。RLT 将反馈放置在编码器–解码器接口，并通过完整历史重放进行训练。 - \*\*T2MLR (Cai et al., 2026)\*\* 将前一刻的中间层表示缓存并注入当前位置的较早层，发现中间层循环可能优于全网络循环。RLT 选择通过\*\*整个解码器\*\*进行反馈，并采用完整 BPTT 训练。 - \*\*Latent Recurrent Transformer / LRT (Huang et al., 2026)\*\* 通过 KV 投影和残差注入复用前一 token 的高层状态，保持 decoder-only 骨架和单前向生成。RLT 则将编码器记忆与循环解码器解耦，并在策略梯度场景中要求完整历史重放以精确复现策略。 ### 6. 连续隐态推理 - \*\*Coconut (Hao et al., 2024)\*\* 在隐式推理阶段将最后一层隐态作为下一时刻的输入嵌入进行反馈。 - \*\*PonderLM-2 (Zeng et al., 2025)\*\* 在预训练中插入隐式步骤，并使用 Jacobi 迭代近似其循环依赖。 - 与上述增加隐式位置的方法不同，RLT 的隐态更新\*\*严格绑定于每个普通 token\*\*，不引入额外的潜在推理位置。 ### 7. 块级与段级循环 - \*\*Block-Recurrent Transformers (Hutchins et al., 2022)\*\* 使用注意力和门控更新持久状态向量，每个循环步骤并行处理一个 token 块。 - \*\*Recurrent Memory Transformer (Bulatov et al., 2022)\*\* 为每个段附加写记忆 token，并将其最终表示作为下一段的记忆输入，通过 BPTT 训练。 - \*\*RLT-2\*\* 与上述工作最接近：它在每 B 个 token 的块边界更新反馈状态，但不使用专用的记忆 token 或独立的状态向量组。\*\*RLT-1\*\*（ B=1 ）则进一步将反馈粒度推进到每个 token。 ### 8. 跨深度权重共享与循环 - \*\*Universal Transformers (Dehghani et al., 2018)\*\* 在深度方向共享权重，并可按位置自适应调整迭代步数。 - \*\*Saunshi et al. (2025)\*\* 与 \*\*Geiping et al. (2025)\*\* 研究了在推理时通过共享堆栈的多次应用增加有效深度（latent reasoning / recurrent depth）。 - \*\*DeepLoop (Li et al., 2026)\*\* 分析了权重绑定循环 Transformer 中的残差缩放问题。 - 上述模型在\*\*每个位置上沿深度方向循环\*\*；RLT 则\*\*跨 token 在序列方向循环\*\*，且编码器与解码器的权重共享是可选的。 Q3: 论文如何解决这个问题？ 论文通过引入 \*\*Recurrent Looped Transformer (RLT)\*\* 来解决固定深度 Transformer 无法随序列长度扩展计算路径的问题。该方法的核心在于将模型层拆分为\*\*并行编码\*\*与\*\*循环解码\*\*两部分，并通过跨 token 的隐态反馈使有效计算深度随序列长度线性增长，同时保持固定的单 token 推理成本。 具体解决方案可分为以下五个层面： --- ### 1. 架构拆分：编码器与解码器的深度分配 RLT 将总层数 L 拆分为 L_E 层因果编码器 E_θ 和 L_D 层循环解码器 D_φ 。 - \*\*因果编码器\*\*：对已知前缀 x_(1:t) 进行并行因果编码，输出位置表示 e_t ∈ R^d ，并构建全局键值记忆（encoder KV memory）：

M(le t)^g = (k_t^g, v_t^g)(j=1)^t
其中 g 为记忆组索引。编码器可在已知 token 上完全并行预填充。 - **循环解码器**：每个 token 依次处理。与标准 Transformer 不同，RLT 的解码器在每一步接收来自**前一个 token 的最终隐态**作为额外输入，形成跨时间步的循环连接。 —- ### 2. 门控合并：编码信息与历史状态的融合 对于每个位置 t ge 1 ，解码器输入 ut 通过门控机制合并当前编码器输出 e_t 与前一个解码器输出 s(t-1) ：

r(t-1) = RMSNorm_s(s(t-1))

gt = σ(W_g[e_t; r(t-1)] + b_g)

ut = e_t + α , g_t odot W_s r(t-1)
其中 Wg ∈ R^(d × 2d) ， W_s ∈ R^(d × d) ， α 为反馈缩放系数。该门控机制控制历史状态进入当前计算的强度。 —- ### 3. 解码器块：双层注意力与状态传递 从合并输入 z_t^0 = u_t 开始，每个解码器层 ell 依次执行： - **因果滑动窗口注意力（SWA）**：仅关注当前及最近 W-1 个解码器位置，形成局部 KV 缓存 C_t^(D,ell) ； - **编码器记忆交叉注意力**：通过查询 q_t^(M,ell) 读取编码器全局记忆 M(le t)^(g(ell)) ； - **前馈网络（FFN）** 与残差连接。 最终输出 s_t = z_t^(L_D) 同时作为： 1. 当前位置的下一个 token 预测输入； 2. 下一个时间步 t+1 的循环反馈状态。 完整的解码器状态为 H_t = (s_t, C_t^D) ，其中 C_t^D 为各层 SWA KV 缓存。 —- ### 4. 随序列增长的等效计算深度 这是 RLT 解决核心问题的关键机制： - **单 token 成本**：每个 token 仅通过 L_E + L_D 个块，计算量与标准 Transformer 同级； - **循环路径长度**：处理 t 个 token 后，隐态 s_t 经历了 t · L_D 个解码器块的连续变换：

Ht = F_t circ F(t-1) circ ·s circ F1(H_0)
其中 F_t(H) = Dφ(Merge(et, s); M(le t), C(t-1)^D) 。 因此，RLT 的**有效计算深度随序列长度线性增长**，突破了固定深度 Transformer 的理论限制，而每个 token 的推理延迟和层数保持不变。 —- ### 5. 反馈间隔的灵活设计：RLT-1、RLT-2 与 RLT-0 论文进一步将反馈频率作为可调设计轴，在并行度与状态跟踪精度之间进行权衡： | 变体 | 反馈间隔 B | 机制 | 特性 | |———|——————-|———|———| | **RLT-1** | B=1 | 每 token 更新反馈状态 | 顺序解码，状态跟踪最精确 | | **RLT-2** | B>1 | 每 B 个 token 的块边界更新一次反馈 | 块内可并行，速度更快；某些任务（如奇偶校验）仍保持高精度 | | **RLT-0** | B=∞ | 移除反馈， z_t^0 = e_t | 无循环，完全并行；作为对照验证反馈的必要性 | 实验表明，移除反馈（RLT-0）会使奇偶校验和 swap-based S_5 任务在长序列上退至随机水平，证明**反馈机制是长度泛化能力的关键来源**。 —- ### 6. 训练与推理的一致性 - **训练**：采用教师强制（teacher forcing）与完整时间反向传播（BPTT），梯度流经编码器记忆、循环隐态及 SWA 缓存； - **推理**：支持 prompt 的并行预填充（先编码器并行，再解码器顺序更新），以及生成阶段的增量解码； - **提示–响应一致性**：无论 prompt 在何处截断，只要从保存的完整状态 H_T 继续，条件分布 p_Theta(x(T+1) mid x(1:T)) 保持不变，确保训练与推理、多轮对话中的状态等价性。 通过上述设计，RLT 在固定参数量和单 token 计算开销的前提下，实现了随输入长度增长的表达能力，从而在奇偶校验、模运算和排列状态跟踪等算法任务上展现出远超标准 Transformer 的长度泛化性能。 Q4: 论文做了哪些实验？ 论文围绕 **Recurrent Looped Transformer (RLT)** 的架构设计空间与长度泛化能力，开展了一系列算法任务上的对比与消融实验。主要实验可归纳为以下几类： —- ### 1. 基础设置与对比基线 所有核心实验遵循统一的训练与评估协议： - **模型规模**：基础比较采用总层数为 8、残差宽度 512、FFN 宽度 1,365、4 头注意力的配置。 - **深度分配**：对 RLT-1 测试了五种编码器–解码器拆分—— 4+4 、 5+3 、 6+2 、 7+1 、 8+0 ，并与 8 层 decoder-only Transformer（记为 GPT-8）对照。 - **初始化与数据**：使用种子 42、43、44 进行独立重复实验，共享训练与测试数据流；全局批次大小 512，AdamW 优化器，余弦学习率调度。 - **任务**：在六项算法任务上评估—— 1. **Addition**：1–8 位十进制加法（逆序数字），测试教师强制的答案 token 准确率； 2. **Parity**：3–40 位二进制串的奇偶校验，测试最终答案准确率； 3. **Mod-5 (flat)**：无括号的三元模 5 运算（ +, -, × ，尊重乘法优先级），训练长度 3–39； 4. **Mod-5 (brackets)**：带括号的表达式树，训练长度 3–40； 5. **S5 (swaps)**：基于 S_5 群，仅使用恒等变换与 10 个单对换（swap），训练 32 个操作； 6. **S5 (standard)**：基于 S_5 群，使用全部 120 个置换，训练 32 个操作。 - **长度泛化**：在远超训练长度的测试集上评估（如 parity 测试至 256 位，S5 测试至 512 个操作），每个长度使用 1,024 条共享测试序列。 —- ### 2. 长度泛化与深度分配（核心实验） 该实验旨在验证 RLT 的计算路径随序列增长所带来的外推能力，并探索不同 L_E / L_D 拆分的任务偏好。 - **Parity**：训练至多 40 位。RLT-1 的 5+3 与 7+1 拆分在 256 位上达到 ** 100 ± 0% ** 准确率（所有种子），而 Transformer 仅 50.07 ± 1.63% （接近随机）。 6+2 与 4+4 则在更长序列上出现方差增大。 - **Swap-based S5**：训练 32 个操作，测试至 512 个操作。 4+4 拆分在 256 个操作时达到 ** 97.30 ± 2.76% ** 的最终状态准确率，而 Transformer 仅 0.85 ± 0.30% 。准确率随解码器深度 L_D 增加而单调上升； 7+1 与 8+0 已接近随机水平。 - **Mod-5 (flat)**：训练 5,000 步后， 6+2 在长度 63 的表达式上达到 ** 93.36 ± 5.69% **，对比 Transformer 的 33.20 ± 2.33% 。 - **Addition**：所有模型在训练宽度内均达到 100% 验证准确率，但长度泛化至 32 位时均迅速下降至约 15–17%，未显著优于基线。 —- ### 3. 反馈频率消融（Feedback Interval） 在固定拆分（主要为 4+4 ，并覆盖所有拆分）上，比较三种反馈间隔以量化状态更新的必要性： - **RLT-1 ( B=1 )**：每 token 更新反馈状态； - **RLT-2 chunk4 ( B=4 )**：每 4 个 token 更新一次反馈，块内可并行计算； - 12. On KL-Regularized Policy Optimization

Abstract:Asynchronous reinforcement learning (RL) for large language model (LLM) agents trains one policy on trajectories generated by another: rollouts come from stale checkpoints, and the inference engine’s probabilities differ from the trainer’s even at identical parameters. Standard remedies either clip importance ratios, which biases the update, or, as in GRPO, sample a group of responses per prompt, which is costly when episodes are long. We propose KL-Regularized Policy Optimization (KLPO), a framework that anchors the KL regularizer at the sampler. The regularized improvement step then has a closed-form Gibbs solution, and KLPO fits its log-ratio optimality condition by least squares on the sampler’s own trajectories, so the sampler probability enters through a log-ratio and no importance weights are needed. Profiling out the regression intercept replaces the intractable log-partition function with the signal’s sampler mean plus a sampler-to-trainer KL divergence. For token-level policy mirror descent targets, we show that the resulting gradient can be computed from terminal returns without a critic, via sampler-centered scores or a single trajectory residual, even under stochastic tool outputs. We further prove that independent Monte Carlo estimates of the KL term keep these gradients unbiased, derive the exact KL gap of cheaper top-$K$ and binary approximations, and show that SPPO, GPO, REBEL, and BPO arise as special cases of KLPO. The result is a critic-free update that uses one rollout per prompt and requires neither a learned normalizer nor a group of responses.

13. VIEScore2: Unified Image Evaluation with Spatially Grounded Explanations

Abstract:Existing synthetic image evaluators typically provide only a scalar quality score and do not identify the image regions that support it. Abstract:Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has improved the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs), yet their predictions remain sensitive to task-irrelevant prompt features. 20. WebFovea: When the Model Is Right but the Click Is Wrong — Reliable Round Trips for Vision-Based Web Agents on Live Websites

Abstract:We present WebFovea, a vision-based web agent that placed 2nd in the WebRetriever Challenge 2026 with a final score of 57.0 out of 100. The challenge evaluates agents end to end on Protocol III of the WebRetriever benchmark (arXiv:2607.06118): starting from an entry URL on a live website, the agent must operate the site’s own interface and return a verifiable answer. A capable multimodal large language model (LLM) is necessary for this, but not sufficient. The model’s decisions reach the browser through the harness, the code between the model and the page. At every step, four things must go right: the model’s reply must be parsed into the intended action, the action must take effect on the page, the result must be reported back accurately, and the model must be shown the information it needs. On real websites, many of the failures we observed occurred at one of these four stages rather than in the model’s reasoning. A coordinate-space mismatch placed every click at 3/4 of its intended coordinates; actions on native dropdowns, inside iframes, and in text boxes failed silently; and self-generated chat-template tokens contaminated 4.9% of task episodes. WebFovea hardens each stage and surrounds the loop with guardrails that keep the agent within the rules and its budget. The four-stage view does not depend on the model, although some individual fixes do. Because we used the same model in all four submissions, the rise of our official hidden-set score from 31.0 to 57.0 reflects changes to the harness, up to run-to-run variance on live sites. We describe the design, the evidence for each component (including negative results), a failure analysis, the limitations, and a roadmap that includes routing different steps to different models.
