1. ASCENT: Online Test-Time Training of Long-Horizon Agents via Self-Distillation of Verified Experience

Abstract:A large language model (LLM) agent solves long-horizon tasks through many reasoning-action turns, with one verification signal at termination. Deployed agents face streams of related tasks, making their trajectories a natural resource for improvement. In-context adaptation agents store reflections, memories, or skills as text, so reuse depends on retrieving the right experience and on a frozen policy executing it. We study Online Agentic Test-Time Training (OaTTT), which trains the LLM’s weights on its own execution trajectories during deployment. The agent executes each task once, in one pass over the stream, and the executed trajectory with its verification result is the only learning signal for weight updates that persist across tasks. Directly imitating or reinforcing the generated tokens of this single attempt destabilizes the policy. We introduce ASCENT (Agentic Self-distillation for Cross-task EvolutioN at Test-time), which instead self-distills verified experience. A stable version of the LLM, its frozen initial copy, receives the verified trajectory as privileged information and predicts next-token distributions along it with this hindsight. Distilling them into persistent LoRA fast weights updates the agent for later tasks, without an external reference solution or stronger teacher. By further removing invalid-action turns, ASCENT distills enhanced privileged experience for more efficient execution. We characterize its population target and the limits of sparse outcome selection. Across ALFWorld, WebShop, and AppWorld at varied model scales, ASCENT improves task success and interaction efficiency as experience accumulates, outperforms online adaptation methods, and transfers to held-out scenes, showing that an agent can consolidate verified experience into its weights without a separate training phase or memory retrieval. 2. Rethinking Long-Video Efficiency: A Joint Allocation Perspective on Frames, Pixels, and Front-End Latency

Abstract:Efficient long-video understanding with vision-language models (VLMs) is often framed as selecting informative frames or visual tokens at a fixed native resolution. We show that per-frame resolution can instead be traded for denser temporal coverage, while front-end decoding latency depends on the size of the candidate pool rather than the final token budget. An empirical study across multiple VLMs and long-video benchmarks yields three findings: dense low-resolution sampling outperforms sparse native-resolution sampling at matched token budgets; resolution-sensitive tasks benefit from selected high-resolution frames; and front-end decoding dominates wall time for hour-long videos. Motivated by these findings, we introduce LoHi, a training-free, single-pass framework that combines a dense low-resolution video stream with sparse high-resolution image frames through the VLM’s native video and image pathways. LoHi-Anchor selects high-resolution frames using codec I-frame metadata, while LoHi-SemDiv uses query relevance and visual diversity over CLIP features. Across three long-video benchmarks, LoHi improves average accuracy by 10.6 percentage points over the native-resolution baseline at a matched token budget and by 5.2 percentage points over the strongest prior efficiency method. It also reduces front-end decoding latency by up to 7x on hour-long videos. Project page: this https URL

3. PluginRSI: Recursive Improvement of Agent Harnesses with Reusable Plugins

Abstract:The harness surrounding a language model is a central determinant of agent performance. Recent methods optimize harnesses by searching over complete programs, where individual mechanisms are difficult to isolate and reuse. We introduce PluginRSI, which represents a harness as a composition of atomized plugins and organizes harness evolution around these plugins. Individual plugins are improved independently and accumulated in a shared library, then recombined into new harnesses at each iteration. PluginRSI improves over existing harness optimization methods across software engineering, command-line interaction, and question-answering tasks. The resulting harnesses retain their advantage when transferred to other solver models without further optimization. The evolved plugin library accelerates subsequent optimization from the initial harness, which helps faster and higher convergence on unseen tasks. These results show that accumulating reusable mechanisms provides an effective basis for continued harness improvement.

Q: 这篇论文试图解决什么问题？ 该论文旨在解决\*\*递归式 harness 优化\*\*中因对完整程序进行整体搜索与重写而导致的\*\*机制耦合\*\*和\*\*可复用性缺失\*\*问题。 具体而言，现有方法通过迭代重写完整的 harness 实现来优化智能体性能，这带来了两个核心限制： - \*\*新机制探索受限\*\* 多种交互机制（如工具、角色、技能等）被耦合在单一的 harness 实现中，难以独立地对某一机制进行探索与改进。这种耦合导致优化过程容易重复收敛到相似的设计，阻碍了新颖有效机制的发现。 - \*\*有效机制难以保留与复用\*\* 整体重写可能覆盖或丢弃局部有效的机制，使得历史迭代中发现的改进无法被持续积累和跨候选复用，限制了优化经验的长期沉淀。 为此，论文提出将 harness 表示为\*\*原子化插件（atomized plugins）的组合\*\*，通过围绕插件进行独立变异、评估与共享库积累，实现机制的解耦、持续发现与复用，从而在固定策略模型的前提下提升 agent 的期望任务回报

Zhang et al., 2025）、提示与角色分配（Agrawal et al., 2026b,a）、或可执行组件（Yang et al., 2026b; Yu et al., 2026; Fei et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2026a）进行迭代调整。 - **整体实现优化** 将算法本身或智能体的完整可执行代码作为搜索对象，例如用于科学发现的 AlphaEvolve（Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; 其中： - P 表示选定的插件集合，每个插件 p ∈ P 是一个通过标准化接口暴露的独立机制实现（如角色、工具、技能、记忆）。每个插件由两部分构成：（1）YAML 规范，定义接口模式、配置、提示与元数据；（2）Python 可执行代码。 - W 表示工作流，负责决定插件的调用时机以及输出如何在后续交互中传递。 - H 为确定性编译函数，将 W 与 P 组装为可执行的 agent harness。 这种表示天然支持两种互补操作：**固定工作流与其余插件时单独改进某一插件**，以及**从库中重新选择插件并修订工作流**。 —- ### 2. 基于共享插件库的递归优化循环 PluginRSI 维护一个跨迭代持续增长的**共享插件库** L 。优化从初始 harness H_0 = H(W_0, P_0) （其中 P_0 ⊂eq L ）出发，在每一迭代 t 中顺序执行以下两个阶段： #### 阶段一：插件变异（Plugin Mutation） 该阶段旨在**独立探索新机制**，同时保持 harness 的其余部分完全不变，从而精确隔离单一机制的贡献。 - **并行探索**：每轮迭代并行生成 N 个变异分支。每个分支基于独立采样的任务子集 M （大小为 b ，且包含等量当前 harness 已解决与未解决的任务，以提供多样化反馈）。 - **局部修改**：Proposer 分析当前 harness 在 M 上的执行轨迹与反馈，选择某一插件 p_i ∈ P 并生成修改后的实现 p’_i 。 - **固定上下文重组**：保持工作流 W 及其余插件 P setminus p_i 不变，仅替换目标插件，编译得到

4. OSWorld-Pro: Process-based Evaluation for Computer Use Agents

Abstract:Evaluation of Computer-Use Agents (CUAs) is often limited to the final deliverables they create (at the end of hundreds of steps) and assessed with functional verifiers, as seen in OSWorld. However, such evaluation of end-state performance lacks transparency into how and why agents fail in various tasks, obfuscating critical insight for subsequent improvement. For instance, agents that err during keyboard inputs would require a different mitigation strategy from those that fail to precisely provide click-based inputs on the graphical UI. We introduce OSWorld-Pro: a set of over 300 tasks containing over 2800 subgoals to enable the procedural evaluation of CUAs grounded in over 67,000 human annotations. We use robust human-aligned LLM-Judges to evaluate the fulfillment of OSWorld-Pro subgoals and thereby reveal the progress that models make throughout a series of sequentially dependent subgoals. Our findings reveal that OSWorld-Pro is challenging even for state-of-the-art LLMs, with top performers like Claude Opus 5 achieving only 75.7% vs. 83.4% on OSWorld. Furthermore, we identify critical process-focused failure modes of various models (e.g. subgoal-irrelevant actions and click-based mistakes) to provide insights to improve performance and efficiency of CUAs.

5. Prism: Dynamic Sparse Attention for Native 2K Joint Video-Audio Generation Model Training

Abstract:Natively training joint video-audio generation models at higher resolutions empowers them to learn richer visual details and sharper motion dynamics. However, full attention incurs quadratic cost and, as resolution increases, spreads attention over increasingly redundant tokens, diluting learning signals for informative content and disrupting pretrained priors. Existing sparse attention methods either target training-free acceleration or overlook the unique structure of joint video-audio data, where cross-modal interactions are inherently concentrated around sound-producing regions. To address this, we propose Prism, a dynamic sparse attention framework for natively training joint video-audio generation models at 2K. In particular, Prism organizes the token sequence into spatiotemporal macro-zones, enabling the attention structure to adapt to local content. For each zone, it estimates local information structure via video feature variance along the channel and feature norms from the audio-to-video cross-attention, jointly capturing how visual content varies directionally and how strongly audio influences each visual region. Based on these signals, Prism dynamically assigns a tailored block shape to each zone, applying finer partitioning along axes of rapid visual content variation and strong audio-visual coupling. This encourages tokens within each block to remain semantically coherent, allowing block-level features to capture both visual content and joint video-audio interaction patterns. Prism further adopts a hybrid block selection strategy to dynamically determine per-query sparsity. Experiments show that Prism achieves 2.5$\times$ training speedup compared to full attention, while surpassing it in generation quality.

摘要：在更高分辨率下原生训练联合视频-音频生成模型，使其能够学习更丰富的视觉细节和更清晰的运动动态。然而，完整注意力机制会产生二次计算开销，而且随着分辨率的增加，注意力会分布在越来越冗余的令牌上，从而削弱了对信息内容的学习信号，并扰乱预训练先验。现有的稀疏注意力方法要么针对无需训练的加速，要么忽略了联合视频-音频数据的独特结构，即跨模态交互本质上集中在发声区域。为了解决这一问题，我们提出了Prism，一种用于在2K分辨率下原生训练联合视频-音频生成模型的动态稀疏注意力框架。具体来说，Prism将令牌序列组织为时空宏区域，使注意力结构能够适应局部内容。对于每个区域，它通过沿通道的视频特征方差和来自音频到视频的交叉注意力的特征范数来估计局部信息结构，联合捕捉视觉内容沿方向的变化以及音频对每个视觉区域的影响强度。基于这些信号，Prism会动态为每个区域分配定制的块形状，在视觉内容快速变化和音视频强耦合的轴上应用更精细的划分。这鼓励每个块内的令牌保持语义一致，使块级特征能够捕捉视觉内容和联合视频-音频交互模式。Prism进一步采用混合块选择策略，以动态确定每个查询的稀疏性。实验表明，Prism相比完整注意力实现了2.5倍的训练加速，同时在生成质量上超越了完整注意力。

6. PaLoRA: Paced Low-Rank Adaptation for Continual Learning

Abstract:LoRA-based continual learning methods mitigate catastrophic forgetting through various mechanisms, yet nearly all complement these with small learning rates as a heuristic to restrict gradient scaling magnitude. Such fixed heuristics lack theoretical guidance on how the strength of this restriction should evolve as tasks accumulate. We reveal that even under directional constraints such as nullspace projection, finite-precision updates inevitably leak into the subspace of accumulated prior knowledge along multiple directions. While small learning rates attenuate such leakage, they cannot prevent the accumulated forgetting from intensifying as the effective rank of historical knowledge grows. We show that the optimal magnitude restriction should adaptively increase with this effective rank to balance stability and plasticity, i.e., preservation of previous knowledge and acquisition of new task information. Under an anisotropic leakage model, we derive a pacing law $s^*=\sqrt{R/c}$ that characterizes the optimal scaling of gradient steps, i.e., the magnitude restriction itself, where $R$ is the effective rank of past updates. Based on this insight, we propose PaLoRA, which compresses historical knowledge via adaptive SVD truncation, projects gradients onto the nullspace of prior tasks, and applies rank-aware adaptive pacing. Experiments demonstrate consistent improvements over prior methods, with particularly strong performance in long-horizon settings, achieving substantial gains of 4% accuracy on challenging 50-task ImageNet-A and ImageNet-R benchmarks.

中文摘要

摘要：基于LoRA的持续学习方法通过多种机制减轻灾难性遗忘，但几乎所有方法都以较低的学习率作为限制梯度尺度的启发式来补充这些方法。此类固定启发式缺乏理论指导，说明随着任务积累，限制强度应如何演变。我们揭示，即使在零空间投影等方向约束下，有限精度更新也不可避免地沿多方向渗透到积累的先验知识子空间中。虽然较低的学习率减弱了这种泄漏，但它们无法阻止随着历史知识有效排名的增长而加剧累积的遗忘。我们表明，最佳幅度限制应随着有效排名的自适应性增加，以平衡稳定性与可塑性，即保持先前知识并获取新任务信息。在各向异性泄漏模型下，我们推导出一个节奏定律 $s^*=\sqrt{R/c}$，该律表征梯度步长的最优缩放，即幅度限制本身，其中$R$为过去更新的有效排名。基于这一见解，我们提出了PaLoRA，它通过自适应SVD截断压缩历史知识，将梯度投影到先前任务的空空间，并应用秩感知的自适应节奏。实验显示出相较以往方法持续改进，在长视野环境中表现尤为出色，在挑战50任务的ImageNet-A和ImageNet-R基准测试中实现了4%的显著准确率提升。
