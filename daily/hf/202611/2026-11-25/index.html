1. S-Mind: Internalizing Multi-View Geometry for Spatial Intelligence

Abstract:Spatial intelligence, the ability to reason about 3D space from visual observations, becomes essential as multimodal large language models (MLLMs) move into the physical world. Beyond perceiving each view, it rests on two cornerstones of human cognition: fusing multiple views into one coherent 3D scene and mentally transforming that scene to new viewpoints as reasoning unfolds. Yet our spatial probes reveal that MLLMs largely lack both capabilities, even after spatial fine-tuning. We therefore present S-Mind, which internalizes multi-view geometry within the MLLM through a compact set of spatial tokens. It supervises these tokens to hold the fused 3D scene and re-emits them to re-express this scene from every viewpoint the reasoning adopts. Across four spatial benchmarks, S-Mind improves its Qwen3.5 backbones by up to 22.26 points and surpasses frontier general models such as GPT-6-Astra (+0.45), Kimi-K3.1-Pro (+1.97), and GLM-5.3-Flash (+7.80) in average accuracy. Moreover, the same probes confirm that it acquires both capabilities, reaching fusion and transformation accuracies of 65.11% and 82.17%, against at most 16.02% and 37.28% for the baselines. At inference, S-Mind also runs about 100x faster than a tool-based agent on the same backbone. Instilled through these spatial tokens during training, spatial capability becomes intrinsic to the backbone and persists even when the tokens are withheld, offering a promising path toward spatial intelligence in MLLMs.

摘要：空间智能，即从视觉观察中推理三维空间的能力，随着多模态大语言模型（MLLMs）进入物理世界变得至关重要。除了感知每个视角，它还建立在人类认知的两个基石之上：将多个视角融合为一个连贯的三维场景，以及在推理展开时在脑中将该场景变换到新的视角。然而，我们的空间探测显示，MLLMs在这两方面能力上基本缺失，即使在经过空间微调之后也是如此。因此，我们提出了S-Mind，通过一组紧凑的空间标记将多视角几何内化到MLLM中。它监督这些标记以保持融合的三维场景，并重新发出它们以从推理采用的每个视角重新呈现该场景。在四个空间基准上，S-Mind使其Qwen3.5骨干模型的性能提升高达22.26点，并在平均准确率上超过前沿通用模型，如GPT-6-Astra（+0.45）、Kimi-K3.1-Pro（+1.97）和GLM-5.3-Flash（+7.80）。此外，同样的探测确认其同时获得了这两种能力，融合和转换的准确率分别达到65.11%和82.17%，而基线模型最多仅为16.02%和37.28%。在推理阶段，S-Mind在相同骨干上运行速度也大约比基于工具的代理快100倍。通过训练中这些空间标记的灌输，空间能力成为骨干模型的内在属性，即便标记被保留，仍然得以保持，为MLLMs的空间智能提供了一个有前景的路径。

2. Rewirable Circuits in Transformers with Loom Attention

Abstract:We propose loom attention, which allows a Transformer to dynamically rewire its circuits at every token by interweaving a head’s vertical attention over the outputs of earlier heads (to obtain its query, key and value) with its horizontal attention over tokens. We instantiate vertical attention efficiently with normalized and gated linear attention over a per-token matrix memory, which stores the compressed head outputs and serves as a second residual stream. This yields LoomFormer, a dual-stream architecture that splits control and content between the vector and matrix streams. Across model sizes and training token budgets in language modeling and downstream tasks, LoomFormer matches Transformers trained with over 3x compute and recent residual-stream baselines (HC/mHC, MUDDFormer, AttnRes) trained with ~1.7-2.8x. For example, trained on the same 300B tokens with only 2% extra FLOPs, LoomPythia-2.8B achieves ~1.6x the average downstream gain of mHC-Pythia-2.8B over Pythia-2.8B, and matches (0-shot) or surpasses (5-shot) Pythia-12B. 3. ACAP: Asynchronous Coding Agents for Efficient Robot Control

Abstract:Coding agents can construct and revise robot behavior during execution, but using one agent for the whole robot can delay otherwise independent work. We introduce Asynchronous Coding Agents as Policy (ACAP), an asynchronous framework that organizes one robot as a team of persistent agents. Each member reasons and acts for a configurable control group, such as an arm with its gripper or the torso and chassis, enabling asynchronous reasoning and execution. However, different robot components often have physical dependencies that require coordination. To address this challenge, ACAP introduces a hyper-agent that assigns tasks, coordinates dependencies, and dynamically allocates control between the lead and member agents. This allows independent work to progress concurrently while supporting tightly coupled manipulation when necessary. Across 72 real-world rollouts covering 25 task/platform settings, ACAP reduces task-balanced time cost by 28.2% compared with a single-agent Direct Policy, with improvements across Fully Parallel, Parallel-Sequential, and Collaborative tasks. Across all eight Collaborative settings, ACAP also achieves lower mean time cost than Fixed Delegation, where only member agents can control their assigned groups. An additional 90-rollout study shows lower mean 30-minute-capped time cost than Direct on all six tasks. We further demonstrate mobile manipulation using the same member interface for chassis and torso control. We present the framework and a real-world rollout dataset containing observations, generated programs, actions, and feedback to support research on how coding agents reason and act in the physical world.

4. Antom 3-in-1 Transformer: A Payment Foundation Model

Abstract:We report the development of Antom 3-in-1 Transformer, a payment foundation model that integrates sequence, tabular, and graph representations for payment risk detection. The model contains approximately 7B parameters, with approximately 1.7B activated per task route. Antom 3-in-1 Transformer is pretrained to learn reusable behavioral and relational representations, primarily on Ant International’s internal payment data. In a cross-business transfer experiment, pretraining improves the target business metric by 34.5% over the same architecture trained from scratch. With only 25% of the training labels, the pretrained model outperforms the full-label scratch baseline in Average Precision (AP) and Recall@0.5% on different merchant domains. In a four-month online experiment on Ant International’s billion-scale payment platform, the model reduced the overall loss rate by 37.88% relative to XGB-Online.

5. KMed-31B Technical Report

Abstract:We present KMed-31B, a medical large language model post-trained from an open-weight model for on-premises deployment with limited hardware. Post-training a model for diverse medical tasks remains difficult, because improving one kind of task can lower performance on another. We attribute this to differences between tasks in expected output and available feedback. Medical question answering and diagnosis prediction have reference answers, whereas clinical inquiry has no single correct response and is judged with rubrics. We therefore divide medical training tasks into four categories by these two properties, train one specialist per category with a reward that its feedback supports, and integrate the specialists into a single student with Multi-teacher On-policy Delta Distillation (MOPD$^2$). For clinical inquiry, we build Synthetic Health Dialogue, a simulated corpus of consultations with lay users and clinicians whose patient cases span common clinical presentations in primary care. Relative to its base model, KMed-31B raises HealthBench Hard from 22.95 to 41.87 and HealthBench Professional from 37.92 to 50.20 in non-thinking mode, and from 23.80 to 51.48 and from 41.97 to 59.18 in thinking mode, while its scores on four multiple-choice medical benchmarks are maintained or slightly improved. KMed-31B has the highest unadjusted HealthBench Hard score among the compared models, including proprietary ones, and scores higher on HealthBench Professional than every open-weight model we compare, including models with more than ten times as many total parameters. Applied to another base model, Qwen3.8-27B, the same training recipe also raises both HealthBench scores. We describe the training recipe, data construction, and settings in detail so that institutions requiring on-premises deployment can develop medical models from open-weight foundations.

6. PartAgent: Agentic 3D Part Segmentation via Expert Integration and Closed-Loop Refinement

Abstract:Segmenting a 3D object into semantically meaningful parts requires joint reasoning about geometric structure and semantic context. Pretrained 3D experts provide spatially grounded region proposals, while 2D experts provide semantic cues for identifying object parts. The challenge is to integrate these complementary capabilities into a framework that can both produce meaningful part segmentations and progressively refine them. We present PartAgent, a training-free agentic framework for 3D part segmentation built upon two complementary modules: (i) Structural-Semantic Expert Integration combines regions predicted by the 3D expert with multiview semantic evidence from the 2D expert through image-to-surface correspondences, constructing compositional part partitions at multiple granularities. (ii) Observation-Driven Closed-Loop Partition Refinement observes the current partition and its supporting evidence to select targeted inspection, evidence repair, composition, or reselection actions. It verifies proposed changes and uses the updated state and action outcomes to guide subsequent decisions. On PartObjaverse-Tiny-WT, PartAgent achieves 68.41 mIoU, outperforming the strongest competing method by 8.53 points. Across 3DCoMPaT200, PartNeXt, PartNetE, and PartObjaverse-Tiny, PartAgent achieves the highest mIoU on each dataset, averaging 69.36 across the four datasets, 7.28 points above the highest mean among competing methods.

7. PhyCoVE: Benchmarking Physical Counterfactual Video Editing across Diverse Material Dynamics

Abstract:Physical counterfactual video editing turns a source video and a language-specified physical edit at a given execution frame into a counterfactual video that shows how the scene would evolve after the edit. Existing evaluations with counterfactual target videos mainly cover object removal or specific rigid-body motions, although real scenes also involve elastic deformation, fluid flow, and interactions among rigid bodies, elastic solids, and fluids. We introduce PhyCoVE, a benchmark of 533 cases on 110 source videos that covers rigid, elastic, and fluid dynamics in five interaction patterns, with edits that remove an object, change a physical quantity, or insert a new object. For the simulated cases, a language model proposes scene designs, coding agents build them with solvers suited to each material and physically based rendering and then simulate each edit from the source scene state, and humans check every stage. For the real cases, we record continuously from one camera position before and after each edit. We also propose counterfactual fidelity, a metric that applies across materials and their interactions and measures how much of the physical change from the source video to the target video a generated video reproduces. We evaluate seven open-source and commercial video editing models and find that counterfactual fidelity ranks them nearly as human preference does, but even the best model reproduces only about one seventh of the physical change. The models change how a scene looks but rarely how it evolves, whereas a physical edit requires understanding the dynamics that it changes. Physical counterfactual video editing thus remains far from solved, and PhyCoVE provides a foundation for developing and evaluating methods for it.

We present LoopedWAM, a compact WAM that recurrently applies a shared six-block core in each expert, executing 30 transformer layers while storing only 12 blocks per expert. To account for their asymmetric inference costs, the video and action experts can operate at different depths: visual features are computed once per query, whereas action computation repeats at every sampling step. We further propose elastic self-distillation, which trains a single elastic checkpoint, LoopedWAM-E, across action-depth and sampling-step budgets. With only 0.58B policy parameters and no robot pretraining, LoopedWAM achieves 96.1% success on LIBERO, 91.9% on RoboTwin 2.0, and 52.2% on RoboCasaGR1, while using $7.4\times$ less peak GPU memory than Fast-WAM under matched inference measurements. On LIBERO, LoopedWAM-E achieves 96.0% success in 27.6 ms or 95.2% in 24.7 ms on an H100, providing multiple accuracy-latency operating points from a single checkpoint; with two sampling steps and four action loops, it stays within 0.5 points of LoopedWAM on all three benchmarks. Ablations demonstrate that weight-shared depth preserves competitive performance, while aggressive action-depth reduction requires explicit budget-aware training. On a real AgileX PiPER arm, LoopedWAM succeeds in 71.3% of 80 trials across four tasks.

17. Coding4Games: Can Modern Coding Agents Play Modern Video Games?

Abstract:Games, especially modern AAA games, challenge language models to perceive complex scenes, control actions, and make decisions over long horizons. Existing game benchmarks primarily test language models as computer-use agents that query a language model throughout play, incurring repeated inference cost and, in real-time games, acting while the environment continues to evolve. We ask whether coding agents can learn from game interaction to develop standalone game-playing programs that run without language model inference. We thus introduce Coding4Games, a benchmark for evaluating whether coding agents can develop standalone programs that play modern games through visual observations and standard game inputs. Coding4Games has three defining features: 1) Diverse Modern Games, covering 15 games with diverse visual, strategic, and control demands; 2) Development-Test Separation, in which a coding agent uses a sandbox and the standard player interface to iteratively develop a program, after which the selected program is frozen and independently tested without language-model inference; and 3) Outcome and Process Evaluation, which measures not only game performance and reliability but also how agents use feedback, revise programs, and resolve problems during development. Across 15 games, the best program per game outscores the strongest GUI baseline on 13 and ties it on one. The human player outscores all agents on 10 games. We additionally examine the effects of development budgets. Coding4Games evaluates whether coding agents can turn their understanding of complex interactive environments into programs that work reliably on their own.

18. Can an Agent Trust Its Own Zoom? Cross-Magnification Provenance Verification for Whole-Slide Images

Abstract:Visual agents navigate whole-slide images through magnified observations associated with claimed locations. However, a visually plausible observation does not necessarily originate from its claimed location, particularly across magnifications and in regions with similar tissue structures. We formulate cross-magnification provenance verification (CMPV) to evaluate whether visual evidence supports a claimed spatial association, recover the source when possible, and abstain when evidence is insufficient. We introduce PyraFault, a benchmark of 3.06 million coordinate-supervised examples from 2,200 cases across five TCGA cohorts, covering within-context substitutions, absent-source faults, and geometric perturbations. We further propose ZoomTrace, a claim-independent verifier that estimates the observation’s source before evaluating its agreement with the claimed location. It combines physical-scale alignment, visual retrieval, geometric refinement, and reliability estimation using spatially disjoint competing matches. On 503,320 test examples, ZoomTrace achieves 0.969 AUPRC and 0.987 AUROC, compared with 0.789 and 0.903 for Efficient LoFTR. Further evaluations reveal trade-offs in reliability estimation and limitations in vision-language models, cross-dataset retrieval, and calibration. Integrating ZoomTrace into visual agent tool paths reduces exposure to corrupted observations and silent failures, while downstream accuracy improvements depend on the agent and verification threshold.

19. Residual Guidance for Real-Time Interactive Video Generation

Abstract:Real-time interactive video generation allows users to change the prompt during generation to steer the evolving video. However, existing streaming models, which generate videos chunk by chunk, often keep following the previous prompt after a switch. We trace this failure to context inertia, where the previous chunk acts as a strong prior that suppresses the new prompt. Furthermore, we find that this inertia persists even when the cache retains only the previous chunk, yet discarding the context breaks visual continuity, which limits approaches that edit the cache. Rather than editing the cache, we propose Residual Guidance (RG), a simple, training-free method that counteracts context inertia with a correction measured outside the cache. Specifically, RG denoises the re-noised previous chunk once under the new prompt without the cache and adds the resulting residual to the early denoising steps of the next chunk, where its content is largely decided and the new prompt is most suppressed. Across settings ranging from a single switch to long multi-switch generation, RG substantially improves prompt switching capability on existing streaming models, including those with dedicated switching mechanisms, while largely retaining visual quality. As it requires no training or per-model tuning and adds only one forward pass per chunk, RG can be easily integrated into existing real-time video generation pipelines.

20. Align the Pair, Not the Sample: Representation Alignment for Faster and Better One-Step Generato

Abstract:Representation alignment markedly accelerates diffusion training, but one-step generators raise a question that diffusion does not: \emph{which training path should receive the loss?} A one-step generator sees two kinds of inputs. Corrupted real images come with their clean counterpart, which provides an instance-paired target. Samples generated from pure noise have no corresponding real image, yet this is where the final model operates. We study this question on Priming, which trains one-step generators from scratch by reconstructing corrupted images and then matching feature distributions with optimal transport. Aligning the generative path, the natural choice, collapses recall ($0.45!\to!0.15$—$0.25$) in all 17 configurations we test, including five target constructions and loss weights down to $0.05$. Aligning the paired reconstruction path with a frozen DINOv2 encoder instead helps in every setting we test. A short analysis explains this asymmetry: when the target carries no instance information about the input, the optimal aligned feature is constant within each class, so the loss pulls samples toward class prototypes. A control confirms this: aligning the paired path to a random same-class image instead of the input’s own image removes most of the gain. Inside the generator, paired-path alignment creates an interface block through which post-training routes noise, and the image semantics carried through it predict sample quality across 16 models. The resulting recipe, \method, accelerates \emph{both} training stages on ImageNet $256!\times!256$: it matches the 160-epoch baseline with $2\times$ fewer priming epochs, surpasses its final quality with $4\times$ fewer post-training updates, and lowers FID to 1.30 (B) and 1.155 (L) at the standard budget. A joint single-stage variant trains native one-step generators and reaches FID 1.22 with 115M parameters.
